# Cover Letters

**Rules for every letter.** Under 250 words. One specific reason for *this* company. One piece of evidence with a link. Never "I am a hard worker with attention to detail" without proof. Replace every `[ ]`.

---

## 1. General QA Engineer (use now)

Dear [Hiring Manager's name, or "Hiring Team"],

I'm applying for the [Job Title] role at [Company]. [One sentence on why this company: their product, a recent launch, or the domain. Example: "Your move to real-time payments is exactly the kind of system I tested for four years."]

At Tata Consultancy Services I tested core banking and insurance systems for Citi Bank and Royal London. On Oracle FLEXCUBE I executed 200+ test cases per quarterly release across fund transfers, payments and SWIFT messaging, validated results directly in Oracle with SQL, and raised over 100 defects across four releases, with zero critical incidents after go-live. At Royal London I wrote and ran 50+ API test scenarios in Postman and verified data integrity after migrations with SQL.

Since moving to the UK in January 2026 I've been building my automation skills in public. My ParaBank project tests a demo bank across UI, API and database layers with Selenium, REST Assured and SQL, and it found five real defects, including a transfer API that accepts negative amounts: [github.com/Neelima-Gunduboina/qa-portfolio-parabank].

I'd bring careful, data-first testing from regulated banking, and I'm keen to keep growing in automation. I have full right to work in the UK.

Kind regards,
Neelima Gunduboina

## Job-specific letters: the method

1. Paste the advert into the analysis template in [07-job-search-strategy.md](07-job-search-strategy.md).
2. Take the top 3 "must-have" requirements.
3. For each, write one sentence of **evidence** (work or GitHub link). If there's no evidence, don't claim it; mention it only as "currently learning".
4. Fill the template above that matches the role. Keep the company sentence specific, not flattery.
